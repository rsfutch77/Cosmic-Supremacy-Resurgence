### ICO top tips for games designers

#### Sussing out the danger – running risk assessments

- [ ] **TIP-1.1** Consult with external stakeholders, including children, as part of any risk assessment. To do this, you could gather feedback from existing players, carry out a public consultation, conduct user testing or contact relevant children's rights groups for their views. You could consider completing a Children’s Rights Impact Assessment as part of this process.
  - Applies: the game is for ages 13 and up (decided 8 October 2026), and a player aged 13 to 17 is a child under the code.
  - [ ] Ask beta players about who plays (age bands), as part of the DPIA.
- [ ] **TIP-1.2** Assess and document the game’s appeal to children during the game design stage, and with legacy products, to help you decide the most appropriate age assurance measure to put in place. You should also consider if you need to tailor any in-game content or data processing needs for children. Just because the game isn’t aimed at children, doesn’t mean they won’t want to play it.
  - [ ] Document the game's appeal to children: a 2000s 4X strategy game with text diplomacy and four-hour turns. This is the evidence for AADC-2.
- [ ] **TIP-1.3** Regularly review assessments after a game goes live. If you discover unexpected age groups are playing the game, you should make any necessary adjustments.
  - [ ] Review the assessment once the beta shows who is playing.
- N/A **TIP-1.4** Ensure you risk assess any randomised rewards, such as loot boxes, against the Children’s code and the UK Government’s response to their consultation on loot boxes and gambling.
  - N/A: No randomised rewards or loot boxes.

#### Buff your age assurance – know your players’ ages

- [ ] **TIP-2.1** Assess and document how you will identify if UK players are under 18 and work out their actual ages with an appropriate level of certainty.
  - Applies: the game is for ages 13 and up (decided 8 October 2026), and a player aged 13 to 17 is a child under the code.
  - [ ] As AADC-3.
- [ ] **TIP-2.2** Investigate potential age assurance solutions to provide greater levels of certainty, linking back to risk assessments done at the design stage. You should implement your identified age assurance solution across all games, stores or platforms as quickly as possible.
  - Applies: the game is for ages 13 and up (decided 8 October 2026), and a player aged 13 to 17 is a child under the code.
- COND **TIP-2.3** Implement measures to discourage or prevent players from giving false declarations of age. You could introduce a cooldown mechanism that prevents players from returning to a previous page to provide a different date of birth within a fixed time-period. Alternatively, you could explore access to a data-free core element of your game until parental consent is confirmed or age assurance measures are put in place.
  - Applies if an age question is asked at join.
  - [ ] A player refused for age cannot simply join again with another age at once (a cooldown).
